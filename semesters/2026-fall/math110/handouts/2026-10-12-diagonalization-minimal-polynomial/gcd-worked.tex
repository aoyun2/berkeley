\begin{methodbox}{How to find a repeated factor by polynomial division}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.,leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
\ii For $p=x^3-3x+2$ over $\mathbb R$, $p'=3x^2-3$. The first division is $p=(x/3)p'+(-2x+2)$.
\ii The next is $p'=-\tfrac32(x+1)(-2x+2)+0$. Each remainder is a combination of the dividend and the divisor, so a common divisor of $p$ and $p'$ divides every remainder. The last nonzero remainder, made monic, is $x-1$, the greatest common divisor. So $p$ has a repeated root at $1$: $p(1)=p'(1)=0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{methodbox}
