\method{Find a repeated factor by actual polynomial divisions}
\begin{steps}
\ii For $p=x^3-3x+2$ over $\mathbb R$, $p'=3x^2-3$. The first division is $p=(x/3)p'+(-2x+2)$.
\ii The next is $p'=-\tfrac32(x+1)(-2x+2)+0$. Common divisors are preserved by each remainder step; the last nonzero remainder normalized to be monic is $x-1$, the greatest common divisor. In particular $p$ has a repeated root at $1$: $p(1)=p'(1)=0$.
\end{steps}
