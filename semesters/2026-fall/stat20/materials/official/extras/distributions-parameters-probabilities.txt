Distributions, Parameters, Probabilities - Extra Practice
Question 1
Sir Fluffington is a deeply indecisive cat. His owners have lined up 5 identical cardboard boxes, labeled 1 through 5, and every afternoon he picks one completely at random to nap in. \(X\) is the number on the box he picks today.
1. Name the distribution of \(X\) (Bernoulli, discrete uniform, binomial or hypergeometric).
Show answer
Discrete uniform. Every box is equally likely to be picked, and the boxes are labeled 1, 2, 3, and so on.
2. Give the value(s) of its parameter(s).
Show answer
\(X \sim\) Discrete Uniform\((5)\), so \(n = 5\).
Question 2
A bag of Scrabble tiles holds 100 tiles, 12 of which are E’s. You draw 7 tiles to start the game. \(X\) is the number of E’s you draw.
1. Name the distribution of \(X\) (Bernoulli, discrete uniform, binomial or hypergeometric).
Show answer
Hypergeometric. Tiles you draw don’t go back in the bag, so you’re drawing without replacement, and you count how many are E’s.
2. Give the value(s) of its parameter(s).
Show answer
\(X \sim HG(100, 12, 7)\), so \(N = 100\), \(G = 12\) and \(n = 7\).
3. Write an expression for the probability that you draw exactly 2 E’s.
Show answer
\[P(X = 2) = \dfrac{\binom{12}{2}\binom{88}{5}}{\binom{100}{7}}\]
Here, \(N\) is the total number of tiles (100), \(G\) is the number of E’s (the thing we’re counting - so 12), and \(n\) is the number of tiles you draw (7).
\[P(X = k) = \dfrac{\binom{G}{k}\binom{N-G}{n-k}}{\binom{N}{n}}\]

Question 4
A fearsome dragon guards a hoard of 40 golden eggs. 30 are pure gold, and 10 are identical-looking chocolate eggs wrapped in gold foil (given to the dragon by his sweetheart). One night, a brave knight sneaks into the cave as the dragon sleeps, quickly swipes 6 random eggs, and runs home gleefully to examine his haul. Let \(X\) be is the number of chocolate eggs the knight found in his loot.
1. Name the distribution of \(X\) (Bernoulli, discrete uniform, binomial or hypergeometric).
Show answer
Hypergeometric. The knight takes eggs without putting any back, and you count how many of them are chocolate.
2. Give the value(s) of its parameter(s).
Show answer
\(X \sim HG(40, 10, 6)\), so \(N = 40\), \(G = 10\) and \(n = 6\).
3. Write an expression for the probability that the knight escapes with at most 1 chocolate egg.
Show answer
\[P(X \le 1) = P(X = 0) + P(X = 1) = \dfrac{\binom{10}{0}\binom{30}{6}}{\binom{40}{6}} + \dfrac{\binom{10}{1}\binom{30}{5}}{\binom{40}{6}}\]
Here, \(N\) is the total number of eggs (40), \(G\) is the number of chocolate eggs (the thing we’re counting - so 10), and \(n\) is the number of eggs the knight stole (6).
\[P(X = k) = \dfrac{\binom{G}{k}\binom{N-G}{n-k}}{\binom{N}{n}}\]

Question 6
Each time you play a claw machine [https://www.youtube.com/shorts/-YN-W-e6_Tk], it grabs a prize with probability 1/8, independently of other plays. You play 16 times. \(X\) is the number of prizes you win.
1. Name the distribution of \(X\) (Bernoulli, discrete uniform, binomial or hypergeometric).
Show answer
Binomial. The plays are independent, each has the same chance of grabbing a prize, and \(X\) counts the prizes over a fixed number of plays.
2. Give the value(s) of its parameter(s).
Show answer
\(X \sim Bin(16, 1/8)\), so \(n = 16\) and \(p = 1/8\).
3. Write an expression for the probability that you win at least one prize.
Show answer
\[P(X \ge 1) = 1 - P(X = 0) = 1 - \left(\dfrac{7}{8}\right)^{16}\]
Here, \(n\) is the number of plays (16) and \(p\) is the chance that each play grabs a prize (1/8).
\[P(X = k) = \binom{n}{k} p^k (1-p)^{n-k}\]
Question 7

Show answer

Show answer
\[P(X \ge 4) = P(X = 4) + P(X = 5) = \dfrac{\binom{13}{4}\binom{39}{1}}{\binom{52}{5}} + \dfrac{\binom{13}{5}\binom{39}{0}}{\binom{52}{5}}\]
Here, \(N\) is the total number of cards in the deck (52), \(G\) is the number of hearts (the thing we’re counting - so 13), and \(n\) is the number of cards you’re dealt (5).
\[P(X = k) = \dfrac{\binom{G}{k}\binom{N-G}{n-k}}{\binom{N}{n}}\]
Question 8
At Billy’s Goat Yoga, 9 goats wander around the room during class. Each goat, independently of the others, has a 30% chance of climbing onto someone’s back during the “downward dog” pose. \(X\) is the number of goats that climb onto a back during that pose.

Show answer

Show answer
\[P(X = 3) = \binom{9}{3}(0.3)^3(0.7)^6\]
Here, \(n\) is the number of goats (9) and \(p\) is the chance that each goat climbs onto a back (0.3).
\[P(X = k) = \binom{n}{k} p^k (1-p)^{n-k}\]

Question 12
Peter Piper picked a peck of pickled peppers (what? [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Piper]), but apparently a portion of Peter’s peppers were painfully picante! In other words, Peter has a bucket 40 peppers, thinking they all were just pickled and mild, but 9 were actually extremely spicy (unfortunately, they look just like the mild ones). Peter tosses 6 peppers at random onto a slice of pizza and he eats it. \(X\) is the number of spicy peppers on that slice.
1. Name the distribution of \(X\) (Bernoulli, discrete uniform, binomial or hypergeometric).
Show answer
Hypergeometric. Peppers that go on the pizza don’t go back in the peck, so Peter is picking without replacement, and you count how many are spicy.
2. Give the value(s) of its parameter(s).
Show answer
\(X \sim HG(40, 9, 6)\), so \(N = 40\), \(G = 9\) and \(n = 6\).
3. Write an expression for the probability that Peter’s pizza has at least 2 spicy peppers.
Show answer
\[P(X \ge 2) = 1 - P(X = 0) - P(X = 1) = 1 - \dfrac{\binom{9}{0}\binom{31}{6}}{\binom{40}{6}} - \dfrac{\binom{9}{1}\binom{31}{5}}{\binom{40}{6}}\]
Here, \(N\) is the total number of peppers in the peck (40), \(G\) is the number of spicy peppers (the thing we’re counting - so 9), and \(n\) is the number of peppers Peter puts on his pizza (6).
\[P(X = k) = \dfrac{\binom{G}{k}\binom{N-G}{n-k}}{\binom{N}{n}}\]

Question 15
A box holds 1 gold ticket and 4 silver tickets. You draw a ticket and put it back, 6 times in all. \(X\) is the number of times you draw the gold ticket.
1. Name the distribution of \(X\) (Bernoulli, discrete uniform, binomial or hypergeometric).
Show answer
Binomial. Because the ticket goes back each time, the draws are independent and each has the same chance of gold, and \(X\) counts the golds over a fixed number of draws.
2. Give the value(s) of its parameter(s).
Show answer
\(X \sim Bin(6, 0.2)\), so \(n = 6\) and \(p = 1/5 = 0.2\).
3. Write an expression for the probability that you draw the gold ticket at most once.
Show answer
\[P(X \le 1) = P(X = 0) + P(X = 1) = (0.8)^6 + \binom{6}{1}(0.2)(0.8)^5\]
Here, \(n\) is the number of draws (6) and \(p\) is the chance that each draw is the gold ticket (1/5 = 0.2).
\[P(X = k) = \binom{n}{k} p^k (1-p)^{n-k}\]
