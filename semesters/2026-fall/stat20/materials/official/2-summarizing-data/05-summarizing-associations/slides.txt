Warmup Questions
Correlation
If the correlation coeﬃcient for a given scatterplot is \(r = -0.81\), which of the following must be true about the relationship between the explanatory and response variable? (SELECT ALL THAT APPLY)
The points fairly closely follow a straight line
When one variable is high, the other is likely to be high as well
When one variable is high, the other is likely to be low
The relationship between variables is weakened by outliers.
Part (c) is the only correct answer. We know that if we fit a straight line through the points, it will slope downward, but we don’t know that they follow a straight line at all!
Too see why (a) is incorrect, consider these points. x and y still correlate at exactly r = -0.81, but it’s obviously not a stright line.

Simple Linear Model
In a study, volunteers kept a record of all the food they ate. The amount of protein (in grams per week) and the amount of fat (in grams per week) was then calculated for each person. Here is a summary of the entire data set:
Fat (g/wk) |
Protein (g/wk) |
mean = 668 |
mean = 662 |
sd = 65 |
sd = 70 |
Correlation(fat, protein) = 0.8
Calculate the equation of the least-squares regression line to predict protein in terms of fat.
\(b_1 = (0.8) \times \frac{70}{65} \approx 0.86\)
\(b_0 = 662 - (0.86)(668) \approx 87.52\)
\(\hat{y} = 87.52 + 0.86 x\)
So predicted weekly protein intake is 87.52 grams, plus 0.86 grams for every additional gram of fat intake.

Which state has the highest poverty rate? The lowest?
This can be asked without poll-everywhere, without group discussion, just asking students to shout out which states they think will have highest and lowest.
As they answer, ask where they’re thinking those states points are; how they’re using this plot to think about the highest poverty rate and the lowest poverty rate. Point out that these two questions don’t even take advantage of the scatter plot, which allows you to see the relationship between two variables. Each question just deals with the marginal distribution of poverty or graduation rate, which could just as well be two separate histograms, as shown on the next slide.
